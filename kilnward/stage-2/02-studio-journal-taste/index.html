The lead story

Monday, nine o'clock

The wheel class on Monday night is full again, two members want open-studio time on Tuesday evening, and somebody has written "cone 6 glaze, Thu?" on the whiteboard in a pen that is running out. A studio week is not a calendar. It is a set of promises that all lead back to one box of shelves and elements that can only be fired so often.

Bisque goes in on Monday so it is cool by Wednesday. Glazing happens on Tuesday and Wednesday, around the classes. Somebody packs the kiln on Wednesday afternoon, works out what fits and what waits, and Thursday night it fires. On Saturday the members come in to find their work, and half of them ask which shelf it is on.

Kilnward was made around that rhythm. Bookings know when the kiln room is in use. A load plan knows which pieces belong to which member. The firing schedule tells everyone when their work will be cool enough to touch, and the shelf list tells them where to find it. This issue walks through one such week, set in sample data, day by day.

Tuesday · Bookings

A booking week, not a calendar

Classes, open-studio sessions and the hours the kiln room is closed for loading sit on one week, because they compete for the same wheels, tables and shelf space.

Members book open studio themselves and see how many seats are left. When a class fills, the next name goes on a waiting list, and the first person on it is told when a seat frees up, not the whole group chat.

Staff hours for loading and unloading are booked like anything else, so nobody schedules a glaze night on top of a firing.

Wednesday · Kiln loading

Loading the big kiln, shelf by shelf

Whoever packs the kiln sees every piece waiting for this firing, with its maker, its height and the glaze it carries. Pieces are placed on shelves in the plan the way they go in by hand: tall work low, mugs in the middle, test tiles and small cups at the top.

When something will not fit, it is held for the next firing and its maker is told why, before they come looking for it.

Thursday · Firing schedule

The firing schedule everyone can read

Every firing has a load-by time, a start, the hour it is cool enough to open and the hour work is back on the shelves. Members whose pieces are inside are told when it is done.

If an element fails or a firing runs long, change one line and everyone affected hears about it, instead of finding out at the door.

Saturday · Shelves

Whose bowl is on which shelf

Every member has a shelf, and every piece on it has a state: drying, bisqued, waiting to glaze, in a firing, ready to collect. When a piece has sat ready too long, its maker gets one quiet reminder.

Moving your studio across
