What the run showed

  • The one-line prompt produced the familiar template: three feature cards, invented customer quotes, a strip of made-up studio names and made-up prices.
  • The full brief alone already avoided most of that. Plain Claude with good guardrails is far from slop, so most of the gain comes from writing the brief and guardrails.
  • What the process adds is choice: five directions that look nothing alike, a pick made against real references, and iterations that push the winner further than one pass does.
  • Parallel builds converged on the brief's own wording; three of the first four ended on the same headline. The build prompt now tells each build to write its own copy.
